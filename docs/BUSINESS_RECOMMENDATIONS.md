# Business findings → practical recommendations

**Project 1: RidgeLine Outfitters | Business Performance & Inventory Analytics**

This case study uses the validated Gold-table totals and reviewed Power BI visuals to identify where the business could focus next. The recommendations are **proposals**, not changes implemented or financial benefits already realized.

| Finding | Recommended next action | How to measure success | Key guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|
| $307,275.31 excess inventory and $38,165.45 no-recent-demand inventory (combined $345,440.76; ~47.4% of $728,853.20 total) | Rank excess SKUs by inventory value, demand, margin and age; pilot targeted promotions or assortment reductions before blanket discounting | Excess/no-demand inventory value, inventory turns, sell-through, gross margin after markdown | Avoid reducing prices without checking contribution and seasonality |
| Backpacking Packs represent about $72.5K in excess inventory | Review the category's top overstocked products, upcoming demand and purchasing commitments; test limited promotions or transfers where demand exists | Excess pack inventory value, weeks of cover, sell-through | Warehouse transfers require location-level data, which the current Gold planning table does not provide |
| Five products are flagged for reorder | Review supplier lead time, forecast demand and warehouse-level stock before releasing purchase orders; prioritize highest stockout exposure | Stockout rate, days out of stock, service level, reorder exceptions | Product-level flags are screening signals, not ready-to-issue purchase orders |
| $4.92M sales, $2.02M estimated profit, 41.1% estimated margin; category/product ranks differ | Use both revenue and estimated profit to evaluate promotion and assortment decisions; investigate why Granite Pass Technical Storm Shell ranks higher in profit than in revenue | Estimated profit dollars, estimated margin %, category mix, promotional lift | Estimated profit excludes freight, fulfillment, returns and overhead |
| 111 at-risk customers, 49 inactive | Pilot segmented re-engagement outreach with a holdout group, prioritizing high-value at-risk customers | Reactivation rate, repeat orders, incremental net sales, campaign ROI | Compare with a holdout; outreach effectiveness is not established by descriptive data |
| 33.5% loyalty membership and 200 high-frequency customers | Compare member/non-member repeat behavior and test an enrollment offer with a controlled rollout | Enrollment conversion, repeat purchase rate, 90-day retention, incremental estimated profit | Membership correlation does not prove causation |

## Suggested 30/60/90-day decision plan

**First 30 days:** validate SKU/warehouse detail and supplier lead times; rank inventory exposures; define at-risk customer outreach eligibility; agree on KPI baselines.

**Days 31–60:** run a limited inventory disposition pilot and customer re-engagement test; review reorder exceptions with operations and purchasing.

**Days 61–90:** compare pilot results against baselines and holdouts; scale only actions that improve margin, service levels or customer retention without creating new overstock.

## What I would tell stakeholders

> The immediate opportunity is to reduce the capital tied up in slow-moving inventory without creating new stockouts. I would start with the highest-value excess products, validate location-level demand and supplier constraints, and pilot a small set of actions before recommending a broader rollout. On the customer side, I would test re-engagement for at-risk customers and measure incremental results rather than assume every campaign produces growth.
